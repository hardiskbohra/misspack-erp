# Partial purchase-order conversions — acceptance and verification

## User decisions (2026-10-28)

An approved purchase order (PO) may be split into multiple **cash purchase bills** and multiple **purchase bills**. The conversion amount is a part of the PO's gross total, in the PO currency (INR, RMB or USD). A cash bill has no GST: converting 2,500 from a PO of 10,000 leaves a PO claim of exactly 7,500 even if the original PO included GST. Original PO components are apportioned proportionally; a cash conversion removes its share of those components rather than silently retaining GST on the cash bill. Purchase bills retain their apportioned GST and must be checked against the supplier's actual paperwork; the system does not issue a supplier tax invoice.

The user chooses the amount from **each individual payment** to allocate. Do not pick cash or bank payments automatically. For example, from a 10,000 PO with 2,000 cash and 1,000 bank advances, creating a 2,500 cash bill and choosing 2,000 cash plus 500 bank leaves the PO at 7,500, with 500 paid and 7,000 outstanding; the cash bill is 2,500 paid. One bank/cash movement must remain one movement with its original account, currency, rate, reference and attachments, even if split between documents.

## Invariants

- Use the same permission/maker-checker rules as existing purchase documents: only an approved, active PO can be converted; attempts on another vendor's payment, stale sources, cancelled documents, excessive amounts, or nonrepresentable currency slices fail atomically.
- Conversion creates a linked bill, reduces only the **remaining** PO claim and updates the vendor-ledger posting in the same transaction. No new cashflow payment or bank transaction is made. PO remainder plus converted bills must equal its original claim; payment allocations plus unallocated PO advance must equal the original paid amount.
- The PO's original lines/terms and claim are retained for audit. Converted purchase documents and any PO with conversions cannot be financially edited, cancelled or deleted in ways that orphan the link. Allow supplier reference/date to be captured without changing financial amounts. Reversal, where offered, must restore the PO and selected payments atomically without changing bank balances; unsafe downstream links must block it.
- Multiple partial bills may be created against the same PO. For new conversions, keep each original PO line and quantity while proportionally scaling its unit price to three decimals; line gross must equal displayed quantity × scaled rate. Cash bills have no GST, discount or separate charges: their full value is distributed across the original gross item prices. Purchase bills retain the relevant GST, discounts and apportioned charges. Allow a visible round-off caused by scaled-price rounding even above 0.005, preserving the exact selected total; unrelated inconsistencies still fail atomically. Amounts and supplier paperwork must be reconciled.
- Include direct vendor payments, a user-entered opening advance, and cashflow allocations. A partially allocated vendor payment retains one ledger entry, one cashflow mirror and its attachments; a separate document-slice association supplies per-document totals. Cashflow allocations split their native and document amounts proportionally; do not invent an FX rate or create a zero-valued slice. INR, RMB and USD amounts are denominated in the PO currency; existing INR bank movements and foreign rates remain unchanged.
- Model totals, SQL filters and list statistics, vendor ledger/statement, project budget and closure, detail/print/public views, and the individual payment list must agree. Converted documents do not count twice. Explicitly preserve existing full PO-to-bill conversion behavior for POs without partial splits.
- Migration is additive, leaves existing orders/bills and payments unchanged, and must refuse rollback when converted records/splits would be lost. Native MySQL and HTTP checks cover persistence and locking; browser checks exercise manual source selection at desktop and mobile widths. No production database or real payment is used.

## User decision — full quantities and scaled prices (2026-10-29; current)

This **supersedes the bounded quantity-rounding and original-rate policy below for newly created linked purchase bills**. Both cash and purchase bills copy every PO item with its complete original quantity and proportionally scale its unit price. With 1,000 units each at INR 1, 2 and 3 (PO INR 6,000), a INR 3,000 cash or purchase bill has the same three quantities and rates INR 0.5, 1 and 1.5. The quantities on multiple bills are repeated financial references, not separate physical receipts. Existing saved bills are not rewritten.

Cash-bill prices distribute the entire selected amount in proportion to original gross item value, even when the PO includes GST, discounts or charges. Cash bills display no GST, discount or separate charges. Purchase bills use the selected gross amount relative to original PO total to scale each original unit rate, retain line discounts and GST percentages with arithmetic recalculated from the displayed prices, and apportion invoice discounts and charges. GST follows the existing purchase calculation, including the invoice-discount tax ratio. All totals and PO reductions remain exact to three decimal places.

Rates use decimal half-up rounding to three places. Any residual actually arising from that price precision (including consequent tax/discount rounding) is shown in the bill's round-off, even above 0.005. For 1,000 × INR 1, a INR 333.333 bill has rate 0.333, line 333 and visible +0.333 round-off; the remaining INR 666.667 bill has rate 0.667 and -0.333 round-off. Do not hide unrelated source-data errors in that adjustment: missing or inconsistent lines/totals and negative taxable amounts must still fail atomically. Packaging must not automatically assume repeated financial quantities are physical quantities. Preserve payment and reversal invariants, and continue to block unrepresentable **sales** PI-to-tax conversions without changing them.

## Historical decision — bounded purchase quantity-rounding (2026-10-29; superseded for new conversions)

Previously, for a RMB 21,000 PO (10,000 units at RMB 2.1), a remaining RMB 12,000 bill used 5,714.286 units at 2.1, gross RMB 12,000.001 and -0.001 round-off. The then-approved absolute quantity-rounding limit was 0.005 and larger/zero-quantity discrepancies were blocked. The current user decision above explicitly replaces that purchase-only rule; historical results below describe the implementation as it existed at that time.

## Operational boundaries

- **Not deployed or migrated in production.** Back up and verify the database, quiesce writers, and run `php artisan migrate --force` as part of deployment. MySQL DDL may commit independently; restore writers only after the migration completes. The new migration refuses a downgrade while linked bills or payment slices would be lost. Preserve audit logs: reversal requires the audit register and reserves the reversed bill number.
- Reverse linked bills newest first; downstream packaging, vendor collaboration or share links, and missing/modified/additional vendor credits require review before reversal. A linked purchase bill is an internal apportionment, not a supplier-issued tax invoice; reconcile it against supplier paperwork. Repeated financial quantities are not goods received. Unrelated source discrepancies, invalid discounts and unrepresentable FX cash amounts remain blocked for Accounts review.
- Same-PO conversions are row-serialized, and a duplicate automatic bill number is retried up to three times. Some other operations lock purchase documents, vendor payments and cashflow rows in different orders; MySQL can resolve a concurrent cross-operation deadlock by aborting one transaction. Conversion/reversal retry deadlocks, but an unrelated edit might need a user retry. The same-PO and bill-number races above were tested; exhaustive conversion-versus-edit lock interleavings were not.
