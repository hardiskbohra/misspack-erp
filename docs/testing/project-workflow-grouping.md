# Workflow rule grouping and duplicate prevention

Catalogue: IT-WORKFLOW-009.

## Acceptance (2026-11-01)

- A workflow has project-wide controls and independent per-product controls. Repeating the same rule for different products is not a duplicate and must not be deleted or merged: approvals, bypasses, progress and linked actions remain specific to their product and current source evidence.
- Group the history by stage and then by distinct workflow rule, with an honest count of individual product/project checks. Every product-specific history gate and operational-readiness card must show both the rule name and its own product name and stable project-product ID, including when names are equal; project-wide gates must instead be labelled as project-level. Action cards also identify their product without truncating it. Each existing step retains its own status, bypass and audit history. Long projects should not display a hundred indistinguishable top-level rules.
- The Workflow tab badge combines distinct open rules and active exceptions rather than counting each product check as a separate rule. Expose its rule, check and exception breakdown as accessible explanatory text; do not hide incomplete product work or imply that one product's bypass clears another.
- Repeated and concurrent workflow synchronization must not create two records for the same workflow, product and rule. Take the project lock before synchronizing and use current locking reads so a concurrent MySQL repeatable-read snapshot cannot create a second copy after waiting. Preserve existing step statuses, notes, approvals and bypasses. Do not delete or silently merge pre-existing historical duplicates.
- If existing duplicate records are encountered, surface a clear warning for administrator review and preserve their evidence rather than silently dropping them or misrepresenting their completion. Use fictitious data for all checks.
