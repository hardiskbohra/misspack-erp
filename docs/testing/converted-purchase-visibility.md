# Fully converted purchase orders: listing and vendor statements

## Acceptance — UT-PURCHASE-004, IT-PURCHASE-012 and IT-STATEMENT-008

A purchase order is fully converted when it has a legacy `converted_invoice_id`, or when at least one partial cash/purchase-bill conversion has consumed its entire remaining `total_amount`. A PO with ₹10,000 original value and ₹2,500 billed is still open at ₹7,500; it stays visible until that remainder has been billed. An unconverted zero-value order is not a converted order. Bills are never hidden by this rule. If the latest linked bill is reversed and the PO has a remaining amount again, it reappears immediately.

- The purchase list hides fully converted orders by default. The existing filter drawer offers hide (default), include, and only converted orders. Search, vendor, currency, status/type, date, chips, counts, pagination and filtered totals continue to work together. When converted orders are shown they are clearly identified; their original amount or retained payments must not be counted again in aggregate figures alongside their bills.
- Vendor statements omit the fully converted PO's **order credit** only. Partially converted and unconverted PO credits, purchase bills, payments/debits (even if still linked to the PO), and unrelated manual ledger entries remain. A cashflow-linked credit, an unlinked credit, or a credit linked to another vendor's order must not be mistaken for an automatic PO posting. This applies to old/stale ledger rows as well as new conversions without deleting or rewriting ledger history.
- Statement body, period opening balance, totals, ageing, currency choices/other-currency summary, statement list, print/PDF fallback and public share use the same visibility decision. Other-currency and opening figures are not polluted by hidden stale rows; a currency containing genuine payments remains available. The vendor opening balance uses the same credit-minus-debit sign as its running balance. Client statements are unchanged.
- Classification and filters must agree with native three-decimal MySQL values. Reads must not mutate purchases, payments or ledger rows. Reversal restores the open order and its remaining credit; deleting a legacy bill (individually or via draft bulk deletion) restores the order posting without overwriting manually linked adjustments. A document with an opening advance or other active payment cannot be deleted until the amounts are resolved; this prevents a carried advance from disappearing. The list's paid/outstanding tiles remain allocation-based figures for the filtered documents, not a net vendor ledger balance; a real payment retained on a hidden order still appears in the vendor statement.
